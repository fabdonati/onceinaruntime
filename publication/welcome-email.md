# Subscriber welcome

Subject: Welcome to Once in a Runtime

Thanks for subscribing to Once in a Runtime. Before we get into the code, here’s the story behind the name.

Dream Theater became my favourite band when I was in high school, after my best friend, who played guitar, introduced me to them. *A Change of Seasons* was my favourite song, and their live album [*Once in a LIVEtime*](https://dreamtheater.net/discography-item/once-in-a-livetime/) eventually lent this blog its name, with a small adjustment for the programming side of things.

The live part of my own story is still unfinished, though. The only time I managed to get tickets was for a concert in Rome, which I ended up missing, so I’m hoping another opportunity comes along, ideally one that includes actually being there.

There’s a programming joke in the name too, because a bug that shows up once and then refuses to return can leave you wishing for a repeat performance. After a few reruns and some extra logging, everything appears to work perfectly, leaving you to investigate an incident the software now politely denies ever happened.

That curiosity is what I want to bring here. A new language feature, a design decision or an unfamiliar piece of technology can be a good excuse to open an editor and see what happens, then follow the questions that come up along the way. Sometimes the interesting part is how something works; sometimes it’s the problem it helps solve, the tradeoffs it introduces or the surprising behaviour you only notice once you try it.

Programming and systems will be the common thread, with room for new technology, numerical software, trading systems and whatever else proves worth a closer look. Some explorations will involve performance measurements, while others will follow a working example, a design question or a useful discovery. I’ll share the examples and reasoning behind them, including the awkward details that tend to get left out of the introduction.

I’m Fabrizio Donati, a software engineer working with simulation and numerical software, and this is a personal publication independent of my employer. Articles are free, with code and reproduction instructions available on [GitHub](https://github.com/fabdonati/onceinaruntime).

Fabrizio
